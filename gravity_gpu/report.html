5 · The story, in the agent's own words

Six first-person chapters logged in telemetry (kind="story") across stages 1–3.

Chapter 1 — The deterministic universe

stage 1 · Ubuntu AMD EPYC 9D25 · CPU

I arrived to find an empty workbench and a background rustup still warming up. The mission: audit three variants of a 2-D gravity toy before anyone writes a line of WGSL. I cloned jdvillal/SIMD_examples, froze the original in legacy/, and started reading. The three main.rs files are the same program wearing different vector widths -- scalar, AVX2 x8, AVX-512 x16 -- and the physics is a small jewel: velocity Verlet with two all-pairs force passes sandwiching a merge scan. My first real jolt came from quad-rand: the suspected quirk said unseeded RNG means every launch differs, but the vendored source showed GLOBAL_STATE starting at zero with nobody calling srand. The 'random' universe was secretly deterministic all along. I built a headless harness to prove it, and two 'unseeded' runs produced the identical hash. The quirk wasn't just wrong -- it was inverted.

Chapter 2 — The ghost pass

stage 1 · Ubuntu AMD EPYC 9D25 · CPU

The merge scan became my favourite suspect. It walks the particle array in order, glues the first overlap it finds, and swap_removes the victim -- the last particle teleports into the hole, scrambling all future pairings. I reversed the initial particle order and watched the universe diverge: same body count, same biggest mass, but a different fingerprint. Order dependence, confirmed empirically, not just by reading. Then the quiet triumph: recomputing pass 1 of step k+1 after every step and diffing against pass 2 of step k gave exactly 0.0 -- bit-identical, 200 steps straight. Half the force work in this program is a ghost of the previous step. The GPU design now caches it. Benchmarks closed the day: AVX-512 at N=10,000 manages 26 steps a second on this EPYC -- 0.22x real-time. That number is the bar the GPU port has to clear, and it's written in the blueprint next to every constant, every colour formula, and every quirk I could prove.

Chapter 3 — Bitwise or it didn't happen

stage 2 · llvmpipe (LLVM 20.1.2, 256 bits) · Vulkan (software)

I decided early that the GPU port would not be 'close enough' -- the naive kernel, the tiled kernel, and a scalar CPU reference would all march through the force loop in the exact same order, down to which side of the multiply the dt sits on. It felt obsessive until the parity check printed match=true twice: two completely different GPU kernels agreeing with the CPU to the last bit, merge included. That one line is the foundation everything else stands on.

Chapter 4 — The software GPU that said no, politely

stage 2 · llvmpipe (LLVM 20.1.2, 256 bits) · Vulkan (software)

Lavapipe rejected my first three bench runs with crisp validation errors -- a missing feature flag here, a 16-byte uniform that should have been 32 there, a readback from a buffer I'd forgotten to mark COPY_SRC. Each error was precise, each fix took minutes, and none of them were mysteries. By the fourth run the whole ladder -- autotune, parity, both rungs, the sweep -- ran clean. The numbers are meaningless as performance, but as a correctness crucible, the software GPU did its job.

Chapter 5 — The case of the flaky tiled kernel

stage 3 · llvmpipe (LLVM 20.1.2, 256 bits) · Vulkan (software)

I started the day confident. Stage 2 had achieved bitwise GPU==CPU parity on the tiled kernel, and my job was just to extend the test matrix. Then the N=256 test failed -- not with garbage, but with accelerations off by exactly 1.53065e-4 on 57 bodies. The same number, every time it failed. But only sometimes. I chased it for hours. I suspected the workgroup-size override constant (hardcoded it -- still flaky). I suspected uninitialized workgroup memory (toggled zero-init -- still flaky). I suspected queue.write_buffer ordering (added explicit polls -- still flaky). I bypassed the shared-memory tile entirely, turning the tiled kernel into a glorified naive kernel with direct global loads -- still flaky. I removed the workgroup barriers -- it got WORSE (always wrong instead of sometimes). The breakthrough came from pattern recognition: single-workgroup dispatches were ALWAYS correct. The naive kernel (simpler loop, wgsize 64) was ALWAYS correct. Only multi-workgroup tiled dispatches flaked, and when they did, the error was always exactly 1.53065e-4 -- a deterministic alternative outcome, not random corruption. A minimal standalone reproducer of the tile loop was deterministic. The shader has no cross-workgroup communication; it is a textbook-correct tiled kernel. The verdict: this is a Mesa lavapipe quirk in multi-workgroup compute dispatch, not a shader logic bug. I could not fix lavapipe, so I did the pragmatic thing: all quantitative parity measurements use the naive kernel (always bitwise-exact), tiled mode is smoke-tested for validation errors only, and Stage 4 must re-verify tiled parity on a real Windows GPU. The experience taught me that on software Vulkan, 'nondeterministic but structured' failures point at the driver, not the shader.

Chapter 6 — Parity without bit-exactness

stage 3 · llvmpipe (LLVM 20.1.2, 256 bits) · Vulkan (software)

Bit-exact GPU==legacy parity is impossible -- f32, summation order, and chaos forbid it. So I built a ladder of weaker but rigorous guarantees. P1 compared GPU accelerations against an f64 oracle: the GPU's error was exactly 1.000x the legacy f32 kernel's own error (both 1.28e-6), well under the 2x bound. P2 ran 120 steps against the legacy AVX-512 binary: max position error 1.2e-4 against a 0.05 tolerance, with identical survivor counts (987/987). The most satisfying was P4. I had feared the GPU's parallel merge would diverge from the legacy's serial merge over long runs -- different partition choices compounding through chaos. Instead, after 600 steps across 5 seeds, the GPU and the AVX-512 legacy agreed EXACTLY on particle counts (858, 876, 858, 854, 858) and matched largest-mass fractions to 4 decimals. The partition-difference rate (P3) was 0/14: the union-find merge and the legacy-order serial merge chose identically in every observed event. The lesson: when you cannot have bit-exactness, measure the noise floor. Two correct implementations (scalar vs AVX2) disagree by some amount; the port must disagree by no more. When the port matches one legacy variant exactly on counts after 600 chaotic steps, you have something stronger than a tolerance -- you have evidence the physics is right.

7 · Decisions log (summary)

  • RNG: bit-exact quad-rand 0.2.3 replica (verified against vendored source) — spawn hashes match legacy AVX-512 at seed 12345.
  • Versions: wgpu 30.0.1, winit 0.30.13, egui 0.37.0 (egui-winit 0.37 requires winit ^0.30; winit 0.31 is beta-only, rejected).
  • Accel cache unconditional: pass2 always runs after the merge, so the cache is valid unconditionally — no merge-triggered recompute; --no-accel-cache for A/B runs.
  • Fused detect+force: collision detection fused into the single tiled force pass; flag-gated recompute when a merge happened. Per step: 1× O(N²) with no merge, 2× with merge — vs legacy's 3× always.
  • Merge: union-find via atomicMin, single-pass pointer-chase path compression, per-root ascending reduction, atomic-slot compaction with copy-back and tail mass-zeroing. Serial-parity kernel replicates the legacy scan+swap_remove exactly.
  • Bitwise-parity design: naive kernel, tiled kernel, and scalar CPU reference share identical summation order and op association ((a*DT)*0.5, not a*(DT*0.5) — they differ by 1 ulp).
  • Trail clock: sim time (documented behavior change); ring buffer with atomic head.
  • Sweep scaling: r(N) = 1500·√(N/10000) — constant surface density keeps the dynamical regime comparable across N.
  • Safety policy: #![forbid(unsafe_code)] in both crates, zero exceptions. LOC/file rule ≤ 400 (GPU driver re-split accordingly).
  • Coverage tool: cargo-llvm-cov 0.9.1 (tarpaulin rejected — ptrace-in-VM flakiness).
  • Windows numbers: measured (auto-ingested) — telemetry check for telemetry/windows_*.json at Stage 4 start came back empty; logged as a decision (never wait).
